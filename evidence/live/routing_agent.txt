Rome, Italy, has a rich and extensive history that spans over two and a half thousand years. It was founded, according to legend, in 753 BCE by Romulus and Remus. Rome began as a small settlement on the Tiber River and grew into a powerful city-state.

During the Roman Kingdom period (753–509 BCE), Rome was ruled by kings. This era ended with the establishment of the Roman Republic in 509 BCE, characterized by a complex system of checks and balances and governance by elected officials called senators.

The Roman Republic expanded its territory through military conquest and alliances, eventually dominating the Italian peninsula and beyond. Internal conflicts and power struggles led to the end of the Republic and the rise of the Roman Empire in 27 BCE, when Augustus became the first emperor.

The Roman Empire marked a period of extensive territorial expansion, cultural development, and architectural achievements, including the construction of roads, aqueducts, and iconic structures like the Colosseum. Rome served as the empire's capital and a center of political and cultural life.

The empire eventually split into the Western and Eastern Roman Empires. The Western Roman Empire faced decline due to economic troubles, invasions by barbarian tribes, and internal instability, culminating in its fall in 476 CE. Rome then became part of the Byzantine Empire for a time before experiencing various periods of control by different powers.

During the Middle Ages, Rome was a significant religious center as the seat of the Papacy. The Renaissance period saw Rome flourish as a hub of art, culture, and architecture, with contributions from figures like Michelangelo and Raphael.

In modern history, Rome became the capital of the Kingdom of Italy in 1871 following the unification of Italy. Today, Rome is the capital city of Italy and a major cultural, political, and historical center in Europe.
